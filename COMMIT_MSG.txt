Let the email wrap, stop the brand rule collapsing, fix rule() markup

CI failed on four findings at 320px. All measured in a real browser against
the real markup rather than reasoned about, which is how the third item below
turned up.

V1 - split 331px and banner 296px against a 288px content box.

The cause is the email address: thai-anh.trinh@cyberskill.world is 55px wider
than info@cyberskill.world at 13px, and white-space:nowrap made it
unbreakable, so instead of wrapping it forced the whole table open. Measured
alternatives, split, against a 288px box:

  current (nowrap)          331   fails
  short email               288   fits, but only exactly
  word-wrap:break-word      306   still fails
  word-break:break-all      288   fits

break-word is the obvious fix and does not work: it permits a break at render
time but does not participate in the table's intrinsic width, so the table
still sized to the largest unbreakable chunk. break-all does.

That distinction matters more than this one bug. break-all converts width
pressure into extra lines rather than extra width, so the overflow is closed
for any address, not just this one. Measured at 31, 33 and 36 characters -
the longer two are real names this company expects to hire - split and
classic both hold at exactly 288px, the email going from two rendered lines
to three. A shorter address would have fixed today and left "your email must
be under about 21 characters" as a rule every future hire inherits.

nowrap stays everywhere else. It is right for a label and a phone number,
which are short and bounded, and wrong for an address, which is neither.

V12 - the ochre rule crushed to 0px in classic and 1px in compact.

Separate bug, not the email. The cell is width:3 holding &nbsp; at
font-size:0, so its min-content is 0 and the browser is free to take the whole
3px back under pressure. It did so even when the table fit. min-width gives it
a floor it cannot ignore.

And one nobody was looking for. rule() built its style attribute in two halves
with width/height attributes in between, emitting

  <td style="height="2" bgcolor="#F4BA17" style="height:2px;font-size:0;...">

The parser reads the first style's value as "height=", drops the second as a
duplicate, and discards height, font-size and line-height. bgcolor still
painted, so the rule looked right and nobody noticed. The pinned-background
test did not notice either: it searched the tag for bgcolor and
background-color and found both. One style attribute now, built once, plus a
test asserting no tag declares an attribute twice.

split keeps its place in the ten. It is not broken - it is the most compact of
them at phone width, 122px tall against classic's 195px - but two columns in
email cannot reflow, because there are no media queries to reflow with, so it
is also the tightest. Its picker description said "You on the left, how to
reach you on the right", which describes the desktop render only. It now says
what the phone render costs, in both languages, so the choice is made with the
tradeoff visible rather than discovered afterwards.

check.py: HIGH=0 MED=0 LOW=0, 70 runs, 87 screenshots. That run included V13,
the stacked-diacritic canary, executing for the first time - ten canary
screenshots on disk confirm it ran rather than passed by not running.
326 tests pass.
