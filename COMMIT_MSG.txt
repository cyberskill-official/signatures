Let the email wrap, stop the brand rule collapsing, fix rule() markup

CI failed on four findings at 320px. All measured in Chrome against the real
markup rather than reasoned about, which is how the third item below turned up.

V1 - split 331px and banner 296px against a 288px content box.

The cause is the email address: thai-anh.trinh@cyberskill.world is 55px wider
than info@cyberskill.world at 13px, and white-space:nowrap made it
unbreakable, so instead of wrapping it forced the whole table open. Measured
alternatives, split, against a 288px box:

  current (nowrap)          331   fails
  short email               288   fits, but only exactly
  word-wrap:break-word      306   still fails
  word-break:break-all      288   fits

break-word is the obvious fix and does not work: it permits a break at
render time but does not participate in the table's intrinsic width, so the
table still sized to the largest unbreakable chunk. break-all does, and lands
on 288. The email now wraps to two lines at phone width, which is the trade.

nowrap stays everywhere else. It is right for a label and a phone number,
which are short and bounded, and wrong for an address, which is neither.
Requiring every future employee's address to be under about 21 characters was
the alternative, and that is not a constraint worth inheriting.

V12 - the ochre rule crushed to 0px in classic and 1px in compact.

Separate bug, not the email. The cell is width:3 holding &nbsp; at
font-size:0, so its min-content is 0 and the browser is free to take the whole
3px back under pressure. It did so even when the table fit. min-width gives it
a floor it cannot ignore.

And one nobody was looking for. rule() built its style attribute in two
halves with width/height attributes in between, emitting

  <td style="height="2" bgcolor="#F4BA17" style="height:2px;font-size:0;...">

The parser reads the first style's value as "height=", drops the second as a
duplicate, and discards height, font-size and line-height. bgcolor still
painted, so the rule looked right and nobody noticed. The pinned-background
test did not notice either: it searched the tag for bgcolor and
background-color and found both. One style attribute now, built once, plus a
test asserting no tag declares an attribute twice.

Worth knowing: split now measures 288 against a 288 box - it fits with zero
slack, so one more social row or a longer name tips it over. And break-all is
the one property here Word's support for is uncertain; if Outlook ignores it
the address will not wrap there, which only matters in a reading pane under
about 400px.
